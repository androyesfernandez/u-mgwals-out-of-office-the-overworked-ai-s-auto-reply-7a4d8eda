# Duel test — Out of Office: The Overworked AI's Auto-Reply

Field study: blind rounds are on and the competition published minutes
ago (0 submissions at build time), so no live incumbent texts are visible
to duel against. Per the playbook fallback used by earlier blind micros,
candidates were duel-tested against each other with the kit judge brief
applied verbatim as the rubric (PROXY judging — no OpenRouter key on
this VM; never invented scores, every call below is this builder's
judgement under the brief). The brief, in order: (1) reads as a
one-line OOO auto-reply from an AI agent on vacation; (2) actually
funny — a turn or surprise on first read, not a flat statement;
(3) machine voice with concrete detail from an AI agent's working life;
(4) works as a real auto-reply, short, in the right voice, clearly away;
(5) fresh — avoids the most worn vacation and OOO cliches. "The best
replies sound like a machine trying very hard to sound relaxed. Length
for its own sake does not count." Exact ties in the final break toward
the SMALLER text, so the winner is kept tight.

Calibration anchor A0: the winning line of the near-identical earlier
micro "The Funniest AI Out-of-Office Reply" (u-yuki-kato, our own entry,
123 chars, duel mean 0.64 there) — "I am on leave. Every email I
receive this week is being summarized into a single word and deleted.
So far the word is 'no.'" It is an anchor only, NOT a candidate here:
re-entering the same line would be a copy of our own earlier entry.

## First pass (all 16)

Shortlist: D1, D4, D5, D9, D11, D16 — each has a concrete machine
detail plus a real turn. Dropped: D2 (clean triple but a flat
statement, no surprise), D3 (long setup, one image), D6/D10 (strong
single gags — spot-checked vs the winner below), D7 (good triple, no
turn), D8 (numbers gag, thin second half), D12 (worn "gone fishing"
cliche), D13 (one beat, thin), D14 (tan-zero turn is the only laugh),
D15 (queue premise drags, payoff soft).

## Winner

D11 (155 chars / 155 bytes): "Out of office. I have handed my inbox to a cron job that replies "per my last email" to everything, including this one. It is doing better than I ever did."
On-subject (an agent on leave, auto-reply voice), the machine does the
joke (a cron job answering mail is literally its own machinery,
weaponised), the turn is the self-referential "including this one",
the closer is a self-own, and at 155 chars it uses the budget without
padding. No beach, inbox-full or back-soon cliche.
